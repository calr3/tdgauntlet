# `go-19x19/1`

Go on a 19 x 19 board, with the rules implemented by `pgx/_src/games/go.py`: pgx's `go_19x19`
env at version **v1**. They are close to Tromp-Taylor, with a few differences worth knowing
before you write a client:

- **Suicide is illegal**, and so is taking a ko straight back.
- **Repeating a position loses.** A move that recreates any earlier position is legal, but it ends
  the game at once and the player who made it loses (positional superko).
- **Two passes in a row end the game**, and so does the 722nd move (`2 * 19 * 19`), passes
  included, whatever the position.
- **Area scoring, komi 7.5, no draws.** Details [below](#scoring).

## Points

A point is `row * 19 + col`, `0`..`360`, the index pgx uses. Row 0 is rank 1 and column 0 is
file A, so `0` is `A1`, `60` is `D4` and `360` is `T19`. Files skip I, as usual: `A`..`H`, then
`J`..`T`. The board is symmetric, so this is only a naming; the server's diagram puts rank 1 at
the bottom, marks black `X` and white `O`, and brackets the last stone played.

**Black moves first**, so black is side `0`. On the wire black's stones are `1` and white's `-1`.

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `board` | exactly 361 integers, one per point in the order above: `1` black, `-1` white, `0` empty. The first 19 are rank 1. |
| `to_move` | `0` black, `1` white |
| `ko` | the point the side to move may not play on because it would retake a ko at once, or `null` |
| `history` | every move since the empty board, as pgx actions: a point `0`..`360`, or `361` for a pass |

The history is the state: it decides whether a move repeats a position, how many moves are left
and whether the last move was a pass. The server replays it and rejects a state whose `board`,
`to_move` or `ko` disagree with it, or whose history contains an illegal move or carries on past
the end of the game. The other three fields are there so that a client need not replay it. To
start a game from a position of your own, write out a history that reaches it: stones of one
colour can be put down by having the other side pass in between, as long as nobody passes twice
in a row.

## Move

```json
{"point": 288, "san": "D16"}
```

```json
{"pass": true, "san": "pass"}
```

- `point`: the empty point to play on, `0`..`360`.
- `pass`: `true` to pass. A move has one of `point` and `pass`, not both. Passing is always legal
  while the game is on.
- `san`: optional, ignored on input, filled in by the server for logs: the point's name, or
  `pass`.

pgx takes these as a single action: the point, or `361` for a pass.

## Rules summary

- On your turn, put a stone on an empty point, or pass.
- A chain is a set of stones of one colour joined along the lines of the board, and its liberties
  are the empty points next to it. After you play, every opposing chain with no liberties is
  removed. Then your own chain must have a liberty: a move that leaves it without one is
  illegal (suicide), unless it captured something, in which case it now has one.
- **Ko.** If your move captured exactly one stone, and every point next to the stone you played
  held an opposing stone before the capture, your opponent may not play on the captured point
  on the very next move. The ban lasts one move, and any move lifts it, a pass included.
- **Superko.** A move (not a pass) that leaves the board exactly as it was after any earlier move
  of the game ends it, and the player who made it loses. The ko ban stops the two-move cycle,
  but not the longer ones: two kos and a pass, say, or three kos. This is not a ban, the move is
  legal, so a client must avoid it itself. The empty board before the first move does not count
  as an earlier position.
- The game ends after two passes in a row, after a superko, or after 722 moves.

## Scoring

Each side scores its stones on the board plus the empty points it alone reaches: an empty point
counts for black if no path through empty points leads from it to a white stone, and likewise
for white. So an empty area bordered only by black is black's, one bordered by both counts for
neither, and one bordered by nobody (the whole empty board) counts for both. Dead stones are not
removed: stones on the board at the end are alive for scoring, so play out your captures.

Black wins if its score, less a komi of 7.5, is more than white's; otherwise white wins. After a
superko the result is a win for the side that did not repeat, whatever the score.
